How to play Chess

The goal: Checkmate the other king: attack it so it has no way out.

Setup

The board comes set up. White moves first, then you take turns, one move each.

Drag a piece to move it: it snaps into its square. Drop it on an enemy piece to capture: the table puts the taken piece on your tray beside the board.

The table doesn't check your moves, just like a real board. It's on you to play them right, or to bend the rules on purpose.

After each move, press Next on the bar to pass the move.

Playing with a clock? Press Start clocks. From then on, Next also stops your clock and starts your opponent's. Run out of time and you lose.

How pieces move

  • King: one square, any direction.
  • Queen: any distance in a straight line, any direction.
  • Rook: any distance, straight across or up and down.
  • Bishop: any distance, diagonally.
  • Knight: an L, two squares then one to the side. The only piece that jumps over others.
  • Pawn: one square forward (two on its first move). Captures one square diagonally forward.

You capture by moving onto an enemy piece's square.

Special moves

  • Castling: once per game, move your king two squares toward a rook, and that rook to the square the king crossed. Only if neither has moved, nothing is between them, and the king isn't in, through, or into check.
  • Promotion: a pawn that reaches the far side becomes a queen, or any piece you like.
  • En passant: if a pawn moves two squares and lands beside yours, you can capture it as if it had moved one. Only on the very next move.

Winning

How it ends

  • Check: your king is attacked. You must get out of it this move.
  • Checkmate: in check with no way out. Game over.
  • Stalemate: not in check, but no legal move. A draw.

To start over with a fresh table, leave the room and press Play again.
