Cards and card templates

Lay out a card template once and every card in the set follows it. Change one and the table has it at once.

Card templatesLay out your own fields, fonts, colors, borders and card back. Cards fill in the fields; the template does the rest.
All your cards in one tableA spreadsheet of the set, a column per field. Tick several rows to archive or delete them together.
Edit mid-gameChange a card while you play and everyone at the table sees the new version right away, without restarting.
Card versionsSave a version of a card and go back to it if the change didn’t work.
Foil finishesHolo, Sparkle, Gold, Galaxy, Prism and more, on any card.
Boards, dice and tokensBoard templates like card templates, dice with your own words or art on each face, and tokens with fields of their own.
Rules in tabsWrite your rules in markdown, split into colored tabs. A Rules button opens them at the table for everyone.
Ideas on index cardsPress Shift+N anywhere to jot an idea down before it goes, and turn the good ones into cards later.

Bring your game in

Most designers already have cards somewhere. Import picks the way in and walks you through it.

Tabletop Simulator decks and card sheetsOne image with a grid of cards on it, like a TTS deck or a print-and-play sheet, cut into separate cards.
SpreadsheetsA CSV from Google Sheets or Excel, one row per card and a column per field, becomes a set.
PDFs made for printFrom Illustrator, InDesign or Affinity: each page becomes a card, with its back chosen from the PDF or added after.
A folder of imagesEvery picture in it becomes a card.
We import it for youStudioSend us what you have (a Tabletop Simulator save, a print-and-play PDF, a spreadsheet) and we set your game up on Prototable.
Read fields from card picturesDesignerTurn imported card pictures into cards you can edit: the name, cost and text are read off each one.

The 3D table

A shared table in the browser with everything a real one has, laid out the way your game needs.

Piles and decksShuffle, draw, deal, browse, peek at the top card, and recall a deck’s cards back to it. A pile can belong to a seat.
LanesRows that line cards up: packed like a queue, or with fixed cells that keep a card where it was dropped.
Mats with gridsPlay mats with your own image, and square or hex grids that pieces settle into.
DiceReal 3D dice from d4 to d20, rolled with physics, plus dice you design yourself.
Tokens and trackersTokens, cubes, meeples, counters, life dials, health bars, timers, and bags you draw from at random.
CamerasSaved views of the table. Look through a camera to aim it, and jump between views with the number keys.
A rulebook on the tableYour rules as a book players open where it lies or read full screen, turning the pages together.
Playing cards and chessA shuffled deck of playing cards, a full chess set on its board, or single pieces, straight from the Add menu.
Edit mode and layersMove and resize piles, lanes, mats and text right on the table, and choose what sits on top.
Arrows, attachments and close-upsDraw an arrow from one card to another, clip a marker or counter to a card, and float a card’s face above the table.

Playing together

Share a link and play. Players join free, in the browser, without an account.

Multiplayer from a linkInvite people with a link or a short room code they type in. Everyone moves the same cards live.
SeatsSet how many seats the table has. Players sit down on their own and can change seats.
Play every seat yourselfTesting a four-player game alone? Fill the empty seats with stand-ins and switch between their hands.
Who can joinOpen to anyone with the link, behind a PIN, or knocking for your approval. You choose in Host.
Public game pagesPublish a page for your game with its art and rules, and let anyone play it from Explore. Its page shows the rooms running now.
Kickstarter and Gamefound badgesStudioLink your campaign, and your game’s page and its card in Browse carry a Back it on Kickstarter or Gamefound badge.
Listed first in BrowseStudioYour public games sit above the rest in Browse, marked Promoted.
Turns and scoresName the steps of a turn and the table shows whose turn it is and what comes next. Counters per seat make a scoreboard.
Chat, pings and a game logChat, point at the felt, pin something you said to a spot, and keep a timestamped log of everything that happened.
Classic tablesTexas Hold’em, blackjack, Hearts, chess, checkers and Crazy Eights, ready to play alone or with others.
Phone and iPadThe table works on an iPad, and on a phone held sideways, with your hand along the bottom and the tools in the header.

Sounds, votes and eyes closed

The things a real table does out loud, and the moments a game needs everyone to look away.

Eyes closedFor hidden-role games: close everyone’s eyes but the players you name, and only the table goes dark for them. A macro can wake whoever holds a card and ask them to choose.
Table soundsDice, shuffles, draws, flips and the turn passing each make a sound everyone at the table hears. Choose which ones your game plays.
MusicPick a track from Prototable’s library to play under your table, and change it from a macro.
Your own soundsDesignerUpload sounds for your game, up to 10 seconds each, and play them at the table or from a macro.
Secret votesDesignerA macro calls a vote: everyone picks a player in secret, and the table sees the reveal together.
NarrationStudioSpoken scenes up to 5 minutes, read to the table while everyone’s eyes are closed.

Macros and automations

Let the table do the chores, so a playtest is about the game and not the bookkeeping.

MacrosOne button to deal, shuffle, draw until a hand adds up, move a lane, roll, score or announce. A macro can ask for a number each time.
AutomationsReact when something happens: a card enters a pile, a number drops to zero, or the turn passes.
A command boxPress / to run anything by name: your macros, and every command that has a shortcut.
Your own shortcutsChange any keyboard shortcut, or pick the Tabletop Simulator layout so your hands already know it.

Playtest sessions

Record a playtest and keep what it taught you, on the cards it was about.

Record a playtestPress Record and Prototable keeps who played, how long it ran, and what each card did.
Goals for the playtestNote what this playtest should tell you, and mark how far you got when it ends.
Notes on cards, mid-gameRight-click a card and add a note, tagged balance, idea, feel or bug. It’s filed against that card.
Hear from your playersWhen a recorded playtest ends, players get a short card of questions: the best moment, anything confusing, would they play again.
Public playtests record themselvesRooms on your public game record as soon as someone joins, so you see how new players get on.
Go back to a momentEvery session keeps the table as it started and ended. Pick one up again from either point.

Review and feedback

Turn a pile of playtests into the next version of your game.

How each card is playingDesignerEvery card’s record across your playtests: how often it came into play, and every note about it, with what’s worth a look first.
Compare layoutsEach playtest remembers its table layout, and Review lines them up: how long games ran and how they went.
Keep or discard changesCards changed during a playtest come back for review, before and after, so you keep the good edits and drop the rest.
Stats for your public gamePage views and tables played on every plan. With Studio, how many different people came, who sat down, who came back, and where they found your page.
